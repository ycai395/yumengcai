Computational Analysis of Tandem Floating Wind Turbines under Coupled Pitch-Surge Motion

Published in Scientific Reports | Equal contribution
Vol. 15, Article 41381, 2025
Coupled pitch-surge motion and angle-of-attack definition
Wake velocity fields under representative motion cases

Overview

This study performed blade-resolved CFD simulations of tandem floating offshore wind turbines and compared NREL 5 MW and IEA 22 MW turbines under coupled pitch-surge motion.

Method

Full-scale turbine geometries were simulated with RANS and the SST k-ω turbulence model. Overset grids were used to resolve rotating blades and prescribed platform motion. Three pitch amplitudes and three surge amplitudes were evaluated.

Performance and Dynamic Stall

Increasing motion amplitude generally suppressed mean thrust of the upstream turbines while increasing thrust of stationary downstream turbines. The upstream IEA 22 MW turbine showed stronger and longer dynamic stall than the NREL 5 MW turbine and, uniquely, increased mean power with larger motion amplitudes.

Wake Dynamics

Larger pitch-surge amplitudes enhanced wake velocity recovery and mixing. Wingtip vortices coalesced into thicker three-dimensional vortex rings and exhibited stronger downstream bending and earlier breakdown as motion amplitude increased.

Publication

B. Xie†, Y. Wang†, Yumeng Cai†, Z.-L. Ouyang, C. Zhu, G. Tan, H. Sun, and Y. Chen, “Computational Analysis of Tandem Floating Wind Turbines under Coupled Pitch-Surge Motion Comparing NREL 5 MW and IEA 22 MW,” Scientific Reports, 15, 41381, 2025.
