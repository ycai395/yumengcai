Overview

This work develops a two-way coupled three-dimensional fluid-structure interaction model of the left heart that includes both the mitral valve and aortic valve. Two pathological ejection fractions, EF = 24% and EF = 40%, are compared to examine how impaired ventricular contractility changes valvular dynamics, intracardiac flow, and energy transfer.

FSI Model

The model combines prescribed left-ventricular wall kinematics with fully deformable valve leaflets. Fluid dynamics are solved using a Cartesian-grid immersed-boundary method and structural mechanics using a nonlinear finite-element formulation, with iterative two-way coupling between the fluid and valves.

Valve and Flow Dynamics

The EF = 40% case produces higher aortic flow and substantially greater mitral-valve opening than EF = 24%, while both cases maintain competent aortic-valve closure with negligible regurgitation. During diastole, the mitral inflow develops a bifurcated jet that transitions into a central high-speed stream and generates intraventricular vortices; this vortex-mediated flow organization is attenuated in the more severely impaired EF = 24% case.

Energy Analysis

The EF = 40% condition requires greater left-ventricular work and produces higher kinetic-energy and power flux through the aortic outlet, together with greater dissipation in the left heart. The results illustrate an EF-dependent coupling among ventricular contraction, valve function, filling dynamics, and cardiac output.
