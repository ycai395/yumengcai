Residual Reinforcement Learning Based on Inverse Kinematic Modeling for Soft Robotic Arm Control

South China University of Technology | Advisor: Prof. Yitong Zhou
Published in ASME Journal of Dynamic Systems, Measurement, and Control, 2025
Soft robotic arm used for modeling and control

Overview

This work developed a residual reinforcement learning framework for improving the control accuracy and training efficiency of a pneumatic soft robotic arm. The key idea is to use an inverse kinematic model as prior knowledge and allow reinforcement learning to learn only the residual correction needed to compensate for modeling errors and unmodeled nonlinearities.

Method

A piecewise constant curvature-based kinematic model was first established to map pneumatic chamber pressures to the end-effector position of the soft robotic arm. The inverse kinematic model was then used to generate an initial control solution for a target position.

Residual reinforcement learning was introduced to refine this initial solution. Instead of learning absolute pressure commands from scratch, the policy generates small residual pressure adjustments around the inverse-kinematic prediction. Proximal Policy Optimization was used to train the policy in a continuous action space.

Results

The inverse kinematic controller alone produced substantially larger trajectory-tracking errors because of simplifying assumptions such as neglecting gravity and payload effects.

By combining inverse kinematics with residual reinforcement learning, the trajectory-tracking mean absolute error was reduced to approximately 4.8–7.6 mm under loading conditions from 0 g to 300 g.

The residual reinforcement learning framework required only 30,000 training steps, compared with approximately 300,000 steps for conventional model-free reinforcement learning, corresponding to about a 90% reduction in training steps.
